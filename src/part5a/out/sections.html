Here's what I mean. Someone plays with a widget on their iPhone home screen, and after just the right sequence of touches, it breaks: the widget turns into an empty box. Bugs like this only show up after a particular sequence of interactions, and today it's usually the users who find them. (Video via @techdroider on X.)

These days, when we need a bit of software, we just ask for it: “Hey Claude, can you make us an RSVP site for the wedding?”
A few minutes later, it's done.
And off it goes, to everyone on the guest list. Then people actually use it. Grandma, on an old iPad, text turned way up. A cousin in Tokyo. Two friends, hitting send at the same moment. Someone on a train, tapping twice.
Each of them finds something different.
And it all comes back to you.
“Um, Claude? Grandma can't find the send button… and my cousin thinks it's on Friday?”
Claude fixes it, and everyone waits.
But what if Claude didn't have to wait?
You'd just say: “Find and fix issues in the RSVP page.”
And it goes and uses the thing itself. Clicking, breaking, fixing… again and again,
until whatever reaches people just works.
